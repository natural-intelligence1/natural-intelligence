This SOP is deliberately high-level. It defines WHO does WHAT and HOW it is
recorded when a human at NI actually becomes aware of an immediate serious
concern. It contains NO symptom thresholds, NO diagnosis rules, NO urgency
scores, NO red-flag scoring, NO referral algorithms and NO clinical triage
criteria — the clinical escalation criteria themselves are for the clinical
reviewer to define and own. The software surfaces reported information only
and plays no part in assessing it (see the standing SaMD boundary and the
static non-monitoring wording on the intake).

## 1. Purpose

To ensure that when a person at NI actually reads or hears information that
gives rise to an immediate serious concern about a client or another
person, there is one clear, human, accountable path — and that nothing in
this SOP turns the platform into a monitoring or assessment system.

## 2. Scope

- Applies to NI staff, practitioners on the platform, and supervised
  students, whenever one of them ACTUALLY SEES OR HEARS the information.
- Does NOT create any duty of continuous monitoring: submissions are not
  continuously monitored, and the platform performs no detection. This SOP
  is triggered only by real human awareness.
- Applies to information from any surface (intake answers, consultations,
  messages, rights requests, support contact).

## 3. What happens on actual awareness of an immediate serious concern

1. The person who becomes aware does not investigate or assess beyond what
   is in front of them.
2. If they believe there may be an immediate danger to life, they treat it
   as an emergency in the ordinary human sense (999 guidance applies to
   people, not software).
3. Otherwise they bring the matter, the same working day, to the
   safeguarding lead (see §5) with the minimum necessary description.
4. The safeguarding lead decides the next step with, where clinical
   judgement is needed, the clinical lead — the decision is always a human
   one, made by named people.

## 4. Who may escalate

Anyone at NI may raise a concern. Only the safeguarding lead (or, in their
absence, the named deputy) decides on external escalation. Practitioners
retain and follow their own professional obligations, which this SOP never
overrides or delays.

## 6. Minimum-necessary disclosure

Any escalation, internal or external, shares only the minimum information
necessary for the specific safeguarding purpose, to the minimum number of
people, and is recorded (see §7). Consent and confidentiality are departed
from only where the law recognises a safeguarding basis, on the
safeguarding lead's documented decision with legal advice where time
allows.

## 7. Audit / logging and incident recording

Every raised concern is recorded: date/time, who became aware, what surface
the information came from, what was decided, by whom, what was disclosed to
whom, and why. Records are kept confidentially, separate from the client's
health record, access-restricted, and retained per the approved adult baseline: normally 8 years after adult
case closure, longer where a legal hold applies (minors: separate schedule
required before any under-18 practitioner-led service).

## 8. Confidentiality

Concerns are discussed only with those in the escalation path. Clients are
informed of a disclosure wherever doing so is safe and lawful, as decided
by the safeguarding lead.

## 9. Handoff responsibility

Escalation to an external service transfers the immediate response to that
service; NI's responsibilities continue only for cooperation, record
keeping and any follow-up the safeguarding lead assigns. Handoffs are
explicit — a concern is never "assumed handled".
